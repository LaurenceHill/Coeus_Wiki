# Disagree and Commit

**Source:** [[Invent and Wander]] — 2016 shareholder letter, "High-Velocity
Decision Making." [[The Bezos Letters]] (Anderson, Chapter 7) reproduces
the relevant material with commentary.

## Summary

Disagree and Commit is Bezos's mechanism for moving organisations forward
in the presence of genuine, unresolved disagreement. The phrase serves as a
verbal shortcut that separates the question of _whether everyone agrees_ from
the question of _whether everyone will commit_.

From the 2016 letter:

> _"If you have conviction on a particular direction even though there's no
> consensus, it's helpful to say, 'Look, I know we disagree on this but will
> you gamble with me on it? Disagree and commit?' By the time you're at
> this point, no one can know the answer for sure, and you'll probably get
> a quick yes."_

Amazon's stated Leadership Principle formalises it: _"Leaders are obligated
to respectfully challenge decisions when they disagree, even when doing so
is uncomfortable or exhausting. Leaders have conviction and are tenacious.
They do not compromise for the sake of social cohesion. Once a decision is
determined, they commit wholly."_

## Why this matters

The failure mode Disagree and Commit prevents is _social cohesion masquerading
as consensus_. In a large organisation without this norm, teams tend toward
one of two dysfunctions: premature consensus (everyone quickly agrees with
the most senior voice in the room, regardless of whether anyone has actually
changed their view) or indefinite delay (no one calls the decision because
no one is comfortable overriding persistent dissent).

Both dysfunctions are expensive. The first produces worse decisions by
suppressing genuine disagreement. The second produces slow decisions by
treating unresolved disagreement as an impasse rather than a normal
condition to be resolved by commitment.

Disagree and Commit makes a third path explicit: you can disagree and still
commit to the execution of the decision you opposed. This requires a prior
norm — that disagreement is _expected_, is _valued as input_, and does not
carry career consequences — combined with a clear moment when disagreement
shifts from input to commitment.

## The preconditions

Disagree and Commit functions only if the surrounding culture is healthy
in three ways.

First, disagreement must be genuinely invited before the decision point.
If dissent is suppressed before the decision is made, Disagree and Commit
becomes coercive rather than collaborative — the person who disagrees has
no real standing to disagree because dissent was never safe in the first
place.

Second, the person asking for commitment must be genuinely willing to be
wrong. _"You gamble with me on it"_ is Bezos's phrasing — the word
_gamble_ acknowledges uncertainty. The mechanism fails if it's used as a way
to override disagreement that the senior person doesn't want to hear.

Third, the committer must actually commit. Disagreeing publicly after the
decision is made — _"I told you this wouldn't work"_ — defeats the purpose.
The Leadership Principle's formulation is unambiguous: _"Once a decision is
determined, they commit wholly."_

## Relationship to Two-Way Doors

[[Two-Way Doors]] describes _what kind_ of decision is in front of the team.
Disagree and Commit describes _how to make and execute it_ in the presence
of disagreement. The two operate in sequence: classify the decision (is this
reversible?), then allocate deliberation depth appropriately, then — when the
decision moment arrives — apply the Disagree and Commit norm to get genuine
commitment rather than grudging compliance.

For Type 1 decisions (one-way doors), Disagree and Commit still applies
once the decision has been made through its full deliberation process. The
difference is only in the depth of deliberation before the commitment point,
not in the commitment norm after it.

## Connection to Fisher's management quality test

Fisher's [[Fifteen Points]] point on management-employee relations (point 9)
argues that the best management creates an environment where employees
at all levels feel that their genuine concerns will be heard and fairly
evaluated. The explicit cultural norm that disagreement is safe and valued —
embedded in Amazon's Leadership Principles — is a version of this. The
difference is that Bezos adds the commitment component: hearing disagreement
is not enough; the organisation also needs a mechanism for _moving through_
it rather than being stalled by it.
