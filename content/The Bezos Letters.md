# The Bezos Letters

**Type:** Secondary source
**Author:** Steve Anderson, 2019
**Full title:** _The Bezos Letters: 14 Principles to Grow Your Business Like Amazon_
**Primary source:** [[Invent and Wander]] — Jeff Bezos's own writing is the
authoritative text. Read Anderson alongside it, not instead of it.

## What this source is

Anderson is a business consultant and risk specialist who spent several years
reading Bezos's annual shareholder letters as a single narrative corpus. His
project was to extract the business principles that drove Amazon's growth
from a 1994 online bookstore to a trillion-dollar company. The result is a
synthesis of the 1997–2018 letters, organised into what Anderson calls
fourteen Growth Principles grouped into four Growth Cycles (Test, Build,
Accelerate, Scale).

Anderson is explicit that these categories are his extraction, not Bezos's own
framework. Bezos never presented his letters as a fourteen-principle system.
Anderson identified the patterns and named them. This matters for how the
wiki uses the source: the principles are Anderson's, the underlying
observations and quotes are Bezos's.

The book is organised around four Growth Cycles:

- **Test:** Encourage "Successful Failure" (Principle 1); Bet on Big Ideas
  (Principle 2); Practice Dynamic Invention and Innovation (Principle 3)
- **Build:** Obsess Over Customers (Principle 4); Apply Long-Term Thinking
  (Principle 5); Understand Your Flywheel (Principle 6)
- **Accelerate:** Generate High-Velocity Decisions (Principle 7); Make
  Complexity Simple (Principle 8); Accelerate Time with Technology
  (Principle 9); Promote Ownership (Principle 10)
- **Scale:** Maintain Your Culture (Principle 11); Focus on High Standards
  (Principle 12); Measure What Matters, Question What's Measured, and Trust
  Your Gut (Principle 13); Believe It's Always Day 1 (Principle 14)

## What it is good for

Anderson is useful in three ways. First, he provides a navigational map of
Bezos's letters: if you want to find what Bezos said about a particular topic
across twenty-one years of writing, Anderson's thematic structure surfaces
the key passages efficiently. Second, Anderson includes substantial
verbatim quotations from the letters, which means the book is a useful
anthology even where his commentary is not the most penetrating analysis.
Third, Anderson situates the Bezos letters in the context of other business
literature — Jim Collins's flywheel concept, comparisons to Thomas Edison
and Henry Ford — which is occasionally useful for cross-referencing.

## What it is not good for

Anderson is a synthesiser, not an analyst. His commentary tells you
_what_ Bezos did more reliably than it tells you _why_ it worked or where
the approach would break down. The wiki's [[Anderson on Bezos vs Bezos on Bezos]] page documents the most important divergences: where
Anderson's framing oversimplifies, inverts emphasis, or loses the
precision of the original. The most important case is [[Successful Failure]] versus [[Big Winners Pay for Many Experiments]]: Anderson
frames the Bezos doctrine as a cultural disposition toward failure
tolerance, while Bezos's primary framing is explicitly mathematical —
asymmetric expected-value arithmetic, not cultural permission to fail.

A second divergence: Anderson's Test → Build → Accelerate → Scale cycle
implies that wandering is a _phase_ (the Test cycle) that precedes
execution. Bezos's [[Wandering]] concept holds the opposite: wandering
runs _permanently_ alongside efficiency, in different parts of the
organisation, on different timescales. Anderson's framework makes
wandering look like a startup activity; Bezos frames it as a permanent
organisational discipline even at trillion-dollar scale.

## How this wiki uses the source

Pages derived from Anderson are marked as such. Where Anderson's
framing is substantially different from the primary source, both are
preserved — a primary-source page using Bezos's own terms (e.g.
[[Big Winners Pay for Many Experiments]]) and a secondary-source page
recording Anderson's reframing (e.g. [[Successful Failure]]).

Pages derived primarily from Bezos's own writing via [[Invent and Wander]]
are considered authoritative. Anderson is used to add context,
illustrative examples, or alternative angles — not to override the primary.
