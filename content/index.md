# Wiki: Investing Frameworks Across Five Sources

A cross-referenced wiki built from five primary sources, organised so that
shared ideas, genuine differences, and the occasional contradiction are easy
to find. Links use `[[brackets]]` and resolve to files of the same name in
this directory tree.

### Selection

- [[Fifteen Points]] — Fisher's checklist for what to buy
- [[Scuttlebutt]] — Fisher's primary research method
- [[Financial Community Appraisal]] — Fisher's theory that price is driven
  by the market's appraisal of the company, industry, and market-as-a-whole;
  the fourth dimension of conservative investment
- [[Scale Economics Shared]] — Sleep's central business-model archetype
- [[Robustness Ratio]] — Sleep's measure of moat strength
- [[Destination Analysis]] — Sleep's terminal-state framing of business value
- [[Working Backward]] — Bezos's customer-back design discipline
- [[Buffett's Four Criteria]] — Buffett's compressed selection framework (1977; reinforced 1978, 1982)
- [[Tailwinds vs Headwinds]] — Buffett's argument for industry choice over operator skill (1977; reinforced 1978–1985)
- [[Concentration]] — Buffett's policy of large positions in a small number of well-understood businesses (1978; financial-strength prerequisite added 1984)
- [[Acquisition Discipline]] — Buffett on toads, princesses, and the two categories of acquirers worth admiring (1981; published-criteria filter from 1982)
- [[Economic Goodwill]] — the difference between accounting Goodwill and the stream of excess earnings a great business produces (1983 appendix; 1985 reinforcement)
- [[Intrinsic Value vs Book Value]] — what has been put in versus what can be taken out (1983; reinforced 1984–1985)
- [[Issuance and Buyback Discipline]] — transact in own shares only when intrinsic value and price are favourably aligned (1982 issuance; 1984 buybacks; 1985 stock-options critique)
- [[Owner Earnings]] — Buffett's preferred cash-flow measure; rejects the "cash flow fallacy" of adding back depreciation without subtracting maintenance capex (1986 Appendix)
- [[Mr. Market]] — Graham's parable of the manic-depressive business partner; price serves you, it does not guide you (1987; extended 1990)
- [[Institutional Imperative]] — four manifestations of the organisational force that causes rational managers to make irrational decisions (1989)
- [[Franchise vs Business Economics]] — Buffett's 1991 formal definition: three-part franchise test, mismanagement-tolerance asymmetry, 25x vs 10x valuation math
- [[Margin of Safety]] — Graham's three words; Buffett's cornerstone of investment success; the purchase discipline that corrects for estimation error
- [[Investment Risk vs Beta]] — Buffett's five-factor real-risk framework; volatility is not risk; know-something vs know-nothing investor (1993)
- [[Circle of Competence]] — Buffett's discipline: know the boundaries of what you understand; size of circle is less important than knowing where it ends (1996)
- [[Insurance Float]] — the mechanics and economics of insurance float; cost of float as the primary metric; Berkshire's structural advantage from permanent capital (1997–2006)
- [[Earnings Integrity]] — option expensing as accounting fiction; Son of Gresham; restructuring charges; auditor capture; derivatives as WMD; Fred Futile dividend-withholding illustration; what Berkshire does instead (1997–2005)
- [[Investment vs Speculation]] — Aesop's birds-in-the-bush formula; investment is what an asset produces vs what the next buyer pays; growth as a valuation component not a style (1999)
- [[Gotrocks Family and Frictional Costs]] — Buffett's allegory for how financial intermediaries extract aggregate investor returns; four layers of Helpers; Newton's Fourth Law; 2-and-20 arithmetic (2005–2006)
- [[Great, Good and Gruesome]] — Buffett's three-category business quality taxonomy: savings-account analogy; See's as the Great exemplar; FlightSafety as Good; airlines as Gruesome (2007)
- [[Leverage and Liquidity]] — Buffett's "Life and Debt" framework: zero-multiplier argument; credit as oxygen; $10B floor / $20B customary cash; crisis capital deployment (2010)

## How to read this wiki

Munger's instruction is to invert. Each page tries to surface not just what
the source claims, but what failure mode the claim is built to prevent.
Where the five sources genuinely disagree, that disagreement is preserved
rather than smoothed over.

Where the wiki has both a primary-source page (e.g. [[Big Winners Pay for Many Experiments]] from Bezos's own 2015 letter) and a secondary-source
page on the same idea (e.g. [[Successful Failure]] from Anderson's
commentary), the primary-source page is authoritative. The secondary
page is preserved as a record of how the idea has been transmitted.
