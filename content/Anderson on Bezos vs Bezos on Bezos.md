# Anderson on Bezos vs Bezos on Bezos

**The tension:** [[The Bezos Letters]] (Anderson, 2019) is
sometimes read as if the 14 Growth Principles are Bezos's
framework. They are not. They are Anderson's synthesis of patterns
he sees across 22 years of letters. With the addition of
[[Invent and Wander]] (2020) — which contains every shareholder
letter Bezos wrote from 1997 to 2019 plus his Princeton
commencement address and curated essay material — the gap between
what Bezos wrote and what Anderson extracted can now be evaluated
with specifics rather than impressions.

## Why this is a wiki entry rather than a footnote

When a secondary source extracts principles from a primary source,
something is always lost in the extraction. With Bezos, the loss
is unusually consequential because Bezos's letters are
unusually disciplined — short, specific, and grounded in
observable facts about Amazon at a particular scale and moment.
Anderson's commentary is longer, more general, and more
prescriptive than the source it summarises.

A reader who studies Anderson without going back to the Bezos
letters will absorb principles that are partly Bezos and partly
Anderson, without being able to tell which is which.

## Three places where the gap matters

### 1. "Successful Failure" softens what Bezos actually wrote

Anderson's chapter on [[Successful Failure]] frames Bezos's
tolerance for failed experiments as a cultural disposition. The
primary source — Bezos's 2015 letter, "Big Winners Pay for Many
Experiments" — gives a much sharper version. See
[[Big Winners Pay for Many Experiments]] for the full passage.

The differences are concrete, not stylistic:

- **Bezos's framing is mathematical.** "Given a 10% chance of a
  100× payoff, you should take that bet every time." Anderson
  drops the math and substitutes failure-tolerance language.
- **Bezos names the wins.** AWS, Marketplace, Prime — the three
  pillars whose returns finance everything else. Anderson
  emphasises Fire Phone (a clear failure) more than the wins,
  inverting the emphasis Bezos gave the topic.
- **The claim is portfolio-level, not individual-level.** Bezos
  is describing the expected-value math of a portfolio of
  asymmetric bets. Anderson's framing makes it sound like a
  principle about being comfortable with individual failure.

### 2. The Regret Minimization Framework is missing entirely

The single most-quoted Bezos passage outside Amazon — the
[[Regret Minimization Framework]] he used in 1994 to decide to
leave the hedge fund — does not appear in Anderson. It's in the
Isaacson introduction to [[Invent and Wander]]. A reader of
Anderson would not learn that Bezos's most famous personal
decision-making tool exists.

This is curious. The framework is well-known, widely
referenced, and substantively connected to several Anderson
principles (especially the high-velocity decisions and
long-term thinking themes). Its absence suggests Anderson was
working from the shareholder letters rather than from the
broader Bezos corpus.

### 3. The "wandering" framing is missing or flattened

Bezos's 2018 letter, which gives [[Invent and Wander]] its
title, makes a specific argument: that *guided non-linear
exploration* is a mode that runs alongside efficient execution
indefinitely, in different parts of the organisation, on
different timescales. See [[Wandering]].

Anderson's "Test → Build → Accelerate → Scale" cycle treats
experimentation as a *phase* — the Test phase, before Build.
This is a more comfortable framing for a how-to book aimed at
small business owners, because it implies a clean linear
progression. But it isn't what Bezos wrote. The 2018 letter
is explicit: AWS, in 2018, was simultaneously a "Scale" business
by Anderson's framework *and* still operating in wandering
mode internally on new product directions. The two modes
coexist; they don't sequence.

### 4. Tactical choices presented as universal principles

Several Anderson Growth Principles read as universal guidance
when, in the original letters, they were specific tactical
responses to Amazon's situation:

- **Free cash flow over earnings** ([[Free Cash Flow Over Earnings]]) made sense for Amazon partly because of its
  specific cost structure and reinvestment opportunities. It is
  better-supported by Bezos's actual writing than most
  Anderson principles, but a reader should still ask whether
  the same logic applies to a business with different
  characteristics.
- **Two-pizza teams** is a specific organisational design
  choice for a software company at Amazon's scale. Anderson
  presents it as broadly applicable; Bezos was solving a
  specific coordination problem.
- **The flywheel** ([[Flywheel]]) is Amazon's specific
  mechanism. Anderson presents it as if every business has
  one waiting to be discovered. Many businesses have linear
  value chains, not flywheels.

## What Anderson does well

This page should not read as a wholesale dismissal. Anderson's
contribution is genuine:

- The reprints of the 1997 and 2018 letters are valuable in
  their own right.
- The pattern-matching across 22 years of letters surfaces
  themes that are easy to miss reading the letters
  individually.
- The chapter on [[Free Cash Flow Over Earnings]] is faithful
  to the source.
- The decision-velocity material ([[Two-Way Doors]],
  [[Disagree and Commit]]) is accurately extracted.

The book's value is as a *reading aid* to the letters, not as a
freestanding framework. Used that way, it is useful. Used as a
substitute for the letters, it gives a stretched and
generalised version of what Bezos actually wrote.

## A reading procedure

For the careful reader:

1. Read [[Invent and Wander]] first — at minimum the
   shareholder letters, ideally also the Princeton speech and
   the curated essays.
2. Read whichever Anderson chapter is relevant to the question
   at hand, treating it as commentary rather than authority.
3. Where Anderson generalises beyond what the letter actually
   says, treat the generalisation as Anderson's interpretation,
   not Bezos's claim.
4. Pay particular attention to the material in
   [[Invent and Wander]] that has *no counterpart* in
   Anderson — [[Regret Minimization Framework]],
   [[Gifts vs Choices]], [[Wandering]],
   [[Working Backward]], [[Six-Page Narratives]],
   [[Missionaries vs Mercenaries]]. These are the places
   where the secondary source materially understates the
   primary.

## Why this matters for the rest of this wiki

This wiki has separate concept pages for the Anderson Growth
Principles ([[Successful Failure]], [[Customer Obsession]],
[[Long-Term Thinking]], [[Flywheel]], [[Two-Way Doors]],
[[Disagree and Commit]], [[Day 1]], [[Free Cash Flow Over Earnings]]). It also now has primary-source pages for several
ideas Anderson covered indirectly or not at all
([[Big Winners Pay for Many Experiments]],
[[Working Backward]], [[Wandering]], [[Six-Page Narratives]],
[[Regret Minimization Framework]],
[[Missionaries vs Mercenaries]], [[Gifts vs Choices]]).

Where both kinds of page exist for the same idea, the
primary-source page is authoritative. Where only the secondary
page exists, the underlying letter is generally consistent
with Anderson's reading, but the reader should still verify
against [[Invent and Wander]] before relying on Anderson's
generalisations.
