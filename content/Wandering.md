# Wandering

**Source:** [[Invent and Wander]] — 2018 shareholder letter,
"Intuition, Curiosity, and the Power of Wandering." This
passage gives the book its title.

## Summary

Bezos's argument that _guided non-linear exploration_ is an
essential complement to efficient, plan-driven execution. Both
modes are required; neither is sufficient on its own. Wandering
is _not_ random — it's directed by hunch, intuition, and
conviction that the eventual prize is large enough to justify
some inefficiency in the path.

The canonical Bezos formulation:

> Sometimes (often actually) in business, you do know where
> you're going, and when you do, you can be efficient. Put in
> place a plan and execute. In contrast, wandering in business
> is not efficient — but it's also not random. It's guided —
> by hunch, gut, intuition, curiosity, and powered by a deep
> conviction that the prize for customers is big enough that
> it's worth being a little messy and tangential to find our
> way there. Wandering is an essential counterbalance to
> efficiency. You need to employ both. The outsized
> discoveries — the "nonlinear" ones — are highly likely to
> require wandering.

## The AWS argument

The 2018 letter uses AWS as the central illustration. Bezos's
direct framing: _"No one asked for AWS. No one."_ The world
was ready and hungry for the offering but didn't know it.
Amazon "had a hunch, followed our curiosity, took the
necessary financial risks, and began building — reworking,
experimenting, and iterating countless times as we
proceeded."

This matters because it complicates the simpler story Anderson
tells in [[The Bezos Letters]] — that [[Customer Obsession]]
means listening to customers. Bezos is explicit that listening
isn't enough: _"the biggest needle movers will be things that
customers don't know to ask for. We must invent on their
behalf."_ The discipline is to combine listening (which is
necessary) with inventing on behalf of customers (which is also
necessary).

## What "guided non-linear" actually means

The word "wandering" can be misleading. Bezos is not endorsing
aimless exploration. The wandering is constrained by:

- **A conviction about the destination.** The team has a
  hypothesis — usually about a customer need that doesn't yet
  exist as an articulated demand — that guides which
  directions are worth exploring.
- **A budget.** Wandering happens within a defined investment
  envelope. AWS, Kindle, and the Fire Phone all had
  significant budgets but they were _budgets_, not unlimited
  permission.
- **Iteration tolerance.** The team accepts that the path
  will involve "reworking, experimenting, and iterating
  countless times." Wandering implies a willingness to reach
  apparent dead-ends and back up.
- **A long enough time horizon.** Wandering doesn't work on
  quarterly cycles. The 2018 letter is explicit that
  wandering is incompatible with seeking instant
  gratification.

## The complement to working backward

[[Working Backward]] starts from a known customer experience
the team wants to deliver. Wandering applies when the customer
experience itself isn't yet known. The two modes interact:

- **Working backward** is the right mode when there is a
  visible customer need that current solutions don't address
  well.
- **Wandering** is the right mode when there might be a need
  customers can't yet articulate, where the team's intuition
  is the primary signal.
- Once wandering produces a hypothesis worth pursuing, the
  team typically switches into working-backward mode for the
  actual product development.

A company that only works backward will miss the
non-articulated needs. A company that only wanders will
under-invest in the obvious wins. Bezos's claim is that
running both modes — and knowing which mode is appropriate
when — is the operating discipline.

## Where this connects to Sleep

Sleep doesn't use the language of wandering, but his
willingness to hold positions through extended periods of
ambiguity — Costco in the mid-2000s when its business model
was being criticised, Amazon during years of free-cash-flow
reinvestment that quarterly investors hated — is the
investor-side analogue. The framework permits discomfort in
the path because the [[Destination Analysis]] gives confidence
in the eventual outcome.

[[Patience as Competitive Advantage]] is what makes wandering
affordable on the investor side. Without patient capital, the
intermediate ugliness becomes uninterruptible pressure to do
something.

## Where Anderson's framing falls short

Anderson's "Test → Build → Accelerate → Scale" cycle in
[[The Bezos Letters]] doesn't capture wandering well. Anderson
treats experimentation as a _phase_ (the Test phase, before
Build) rather than a _mode_ that runs alongside execution
indefinitely. Bezos's actual framing is that wandering and
efficiency operate in parallel, in different parts of the
organisation, on different timescales — not that wandering
ends when scale begins.

This is one of the places where reading [[Invent and Wander]]
directly substantially clarifies what Anderson partially
captures.

## A diagnostic question

If a company claims to wander, the test is whether it has
visibly funded recent projects that:

- Had no clear short-term revenue path.
- Were not asked for by existing customers.
- Required new institutional capabilities.
- Were given multi-year time horizons before being judged.

If the answer is no on any of these, the wandering claim is
rhetoric. AWS, Kindle, and the early Marketplace experiments
all satisfied all four. Most "innovation initiatives" at most
companies fail at least two.
