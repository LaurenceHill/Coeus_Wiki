# Working Backward

**Source:** [[Invent and Wander]] — 2008 shareholder letter,
"Working Backward."

## Summary

Bezos's stated approach to product and business design: start with
the customer experience you want to deliver and work backward to
the technology, capabilities, or skills required to deliver it.
Contrasted explicitly with the _skills-forward_ approach, which
asks "we are really good at X, what else can we do with X?"

Bezos's argument is that skills-forward is "useful and rewarding"
but, used exclusively, becomes self-limiting: the company never
develops new skills, and the existing skills eventually become
outmoded. Working backward forces the company to acquire whatever
new competencies the customer experience demands, however
uncomfortable that is.

## The Kindle example

Bezos uses Kindle as the canonical case in the 2008 letter.
Amazon had no hardware experience. Designing a dedicated reading
device required hiring "a number of talented (and missionary!)
hardware engineers" and learning a new institutional skill. A
skills-forward approach would have ruled the project out — Amazon
wasn't a hardware company. A working-backward approach started
from the customer experience (every book ever printed, in any
language, available in less than sixty seconds) and reasoned
that hardware was simply a step on the path.

The 2018 letter on [[Wandering]] uses AWS as a similar case from
a slightly different angle: AWS wasn't designed by working
backward from a customer asking for it ("no one asked for AWS"),
but it was designed by working backward from a future customer
need that Amazon's leadership had inferred existed.

## What working backward actually requires

The discipline is harder than it sounds because it requires:

- **Conviction that the customer need is real and durable.**
  Bezos: "if we can identify a customer need and if we can
  further develop conviction that that need is meaningful and
  durable, our approach permits us to work patiently for
  multiple years to deliver a solution." The conviction
  threshold is high; without it, the multi-year investment
  doesn't get authorised.
- **Tolerance for working without the existing skills.** The
  team has to operate competently in domains where they aren't
  yet competent. This requires admitting incompetence, hiring
  for it, and accepting a period of below-standard output while
  the new institutional skill develops.
- **Patience in financial markets.** Working backward usually
  means deferring revenue and accepting near-term margin
  compression. [[Free Cash Flow Over Earnings]] and
  [[Long-Term Thinking]] are preconditions, not afterthoughts.

## The connection to "Six-Page Narratives"

Working backward is operationalised partly through Amazon's
[[Six-Page Narratives]] — specifically through the practice of
writing the press release and FAQ for a product _before_ the
product is built. The press release describes the customer
experience and the value proposition in concrete terms; the
FAQ anticipates customer questions and concerns. Both force
the team to make the customer experience real on paper before
committing engineering resources. If the press release reads
poorly, the product idea probably is poor.

## Where this connects to Sleep

Sleep doesn't use the phrase "working backward," but his
[[Destination Analysis]] is structurally the same move applied
to investing. Sleep starts with the destination — where the
business will be in ten or twenty years — and works backward
to the present-day investment decision. The skills-forward
analogue in investing would be "we have data on PE ratios, so
let's screen on PE ratios." The working-backward analogue is
"we have a view of the destination, so let's invest where the
path to that destination is clear."

Both authors are choosing the harder analytical mode because
it produces better answers, despite being less efficient than
the easier alternative.

## Where this connects to Fisher

Fisher's [[Scuttlebutt]] is a working-backward research
method. The investor starts from the question _what would it
take to confirm that this company is genuinely outstanding?_
and works backward to the specific people who need to be
talked to: competitors, suppliers, customers, ex-employees.
The skills-forward analogue would be reading whatever's
publicly available and forming a view from that — easier, but
shallower.

## A diagnostic question

A useful test of whether a company is genuinely working
backward, or only claiming to:

- Are recent product or category decisions traceable to a
  specific customer experience the company wanted to
  deliver, _or_ to existing capabilities the company was
  trying to monetise?
- When the company entered new domains, did it visibly
  acquire new skills it didn't previously have?
- Did the company tolerate periods of incompetence in those
  new domains, or did it staff them only with people whose
  existing expertise transferred trivially?

A company truly working backward will fail the
"capability extension" pattern — its expansions will be
_discontinuous_ with its existing skills, because the customer
experience demanded skills the company didn't have. Most
companies that claim to work backward actually do
capability-driven expansion and apply working-backward
language as marketing.
